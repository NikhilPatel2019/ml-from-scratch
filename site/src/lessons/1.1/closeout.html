Passing tests is the floor, not the finish. This is the part that decides whether any of it stays with you.

You are done when

Not when the tests pass — when you can answer these out loud, without looking anything up:

  1. What does a dot product of zero tell you about two vectors?
  2. Why divide by the magnitudes to get cosine similarity — what does it fix?
  3. Why is a NumPy operation so much faster than the equivalent Python loop? ("NumPy is optimised" is not the answer.)
  4. Where does a neuron use the function you wrote today?

If one of those makes you hesitate, the fix is not to re-read this page. Go back to the demo, move the vectors until the number does what you expected, and try again.

Write your notes

notes.md in the lesson folder holds the same questions. Answer them there in your own words — not copied from here. Rewriting an explanation is the cheapest test of understanding there is, and the things I got wrong section at the bottom will be the most valuable thing in your repository six months from now.

One rule for the whole curriculum

Nobody hands you the answer — not this page, and not whatever model you ask for help. Recognising correct code is a different skill from producing it, and only one of them transfers. Ask for a smaller sub-problem when you are stuck. Ask why your working code is bad. Do not ask for the solution.
