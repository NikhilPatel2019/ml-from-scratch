Explain: **$ARGUMENTS**

The learner is a working software engineer with **no maths background**. They can
program; they cannot read notation they have not been introduced to. Write for
exactly that person.

Rules for the explanation:

- Introduce notation at the moment it becomes necessary, never before, and say
  what each symbol is out loud the first time it appears.
- Prefer a worked numeric example over a general formula. Two dimensions, small
  whole numbers, arithmetic they can check in their head.
- Say what the idea is *for* before saying what it is. Motivation first.
- Where an idea has a geometric picture and an algebraic one, give both and say
  they are the same fact in two vocabularies.
- If something is genuinely hard, or is a convention rather than a consequence,
  say so. Do not let them think they missed a reason that was never there.
- No exclamation marks, no "simply", no "just". None of this is obvious.

Read the lesson's `README.md` and `site/src/lessons/<id>/lesson.html` first so
you build on the vocabulary already introduced rather than inventing new terms.

Do not write any exercise code as part of the explanation. If the clearest
explanation seems to require the solution, you have picked the wrong
explanation — use a different example with different numbers.

Finish with one question that would tell them whether it landed.
